Part 1 · covers how the bench counts a hop

Two clicks from the note you have open

5 min live

Every question starts from a fixed note, usually the latest interview where the theme came up, because that is where Ines is when the question occurs to her. From there the reader may take two steps. What counts as a step depends on the rung: open a folder, click a tag, follow a link, or follow a backlink. Then the bench counts how many notes were reached, how many of them carry the theme, and how many of the theme's notes were missed.

LiveThe two numbers, and why both3 min▶

Recall is the share of the theme's notes you reached: of all the notes Ines labelled with "notification fatigue", how many are within two steps. Precision is the share of what you reached that was about the theme. Recall alone rewards reaching everything; precision alone rewards reaching almost nothing. A good structure gets you most of the relevant notes without burying them in the rest. The bench averages both over twelve questions and also counts a question as answered when its recall reaches 75 percent.

Links count both ways, because the backlinks panel from session 1 makes every link walkable from either end. A note is relevant when Ines labelled it with the theme while writing the vault; the labels are the answer key and are not visible to the reader on any rung.

Self-studyWhat the four rungs hold, exactly3 min▶

Folders only: notes live in folders by client and by kind (Clients/Corvid/Interviews, Reading, Insights, Daily, Admin). Tags only: each interview carries its client tag, and theme tags are applied the way people actually apply them: about two times in three, in three drifting spellings (#onboarding, #onboard, #first-run), so a tag page shows one spelling only. Idea links: the 226 wikilinks Ines wrote by idea, about 1.2 per note: interviews to insights, insights to insights and readings, project readouts to the insights they cite, daily notes to the interviews of that day. Some interviews were never processed and link nowhere. Plus maps of content: twelve map notes, one per theme, each listing the theme's insights, readings and findings readout; insights, readings and projects link up to their map, and so do 70 percent of interviews, the ones Ines got round to processing.

The full rules sit in a comment at the top of the bench's script, and the vault itself is a readable data file next to it.

Bench

Ines's vault, rung by rung

Start on rung 1. Read the six numbers, then scan the table for which questions fail. Move up one rung at a time and say out loud which number moved and which way, before you look at the next. Only then press the two levers underneath: the amber one is a move people make on purpose, the red one is an accident.

Read it in this order. Recall, precision, then questions answered. On rung 1 a reader starting from the right interview reaches 14.3 notes on average and finds 19.1 percent of what matters, with 17.7 percent of the reached notes relevant. No question is answered. Part 2 · covers the ladder's numbers

A few links lose to a folder

5 min live

These are the numbers the widget prints, written down so they can be argued with after class. The ladder is not a straight climb, and the dip on rung 3 is the most useful thing on the page.

LiveRungs 1 and 2: the pile and the misspelled pile3 min▶

Folders put an interview next to the other interviews from the same client, whatever they were about. From the start note a reader reaches 14.3 notes on average, and 19.1 percent of the relevant notes. Three method questions (recruiting, synthesis, stakeholder buy-in) start from a project note in a client folder of three notes and reach two: recall 0.

Tags do better, recall 38.3 percent and precision 32.1, because a theme tag crosses folders. But the tag pages split by spelling, and about a third of theme labels never became a tag, so even the best tag question stops at 63.6 percent. Tags are a filing label, not a connection: the help is clear that the graph view draws links between notes, and the orphan count on rungs 1 and 2 is all 192 notes.

LiveRung 3: why a few good links are not enough3 min▶

Ines's 226 idea links are well chosen: precision rises to 34.5 percent, the best of the first three rungs. Recall falls to 11.5 percent and a reader reaches only 4.8 notes. Each link is a short corridor; two steps along a corridor of one or two links each do not get far. Six of the twelve questions reach nothing relevant at all: their start note links to nothing, or only to a note that leads nowhere near the theme. Twenty notes are orphans.

This is the honest finding to keep: linking by idea does not pay off note by note. It pays off when some notes become crossroads, which is what Luhmann's keyword index and the cards his archivist calls hubs were for (session 3).

LiveRung 4: the crossroads3 min▶

Add twelve maps of content, one per theme, and have notes link up to their map. Now the second step from almost any start lands on a map, and the map lists the theme. Recall jumps to 84.4 percent, precision to 66.6, and 11 of 12 questions are answered. Orphans fall to 5 and the largest connected piece holds 96.4 percent of the vault.

The one failure is "trust and credibility", at 26.9 percent: its start interview is one of the 30 percent Ines never processed, so it links to no map. A map helps only the notes that point at it, which is why session 6 makes "link it up to a map" part of finishing a note.

Self-studyWhat the bench cannot tell you2 min▶

The vault is constructed and its structure was chosen by a person, so the bench compares ways of organising one plausible vault; it does not measure how people actually search, and no controlled study was found showing that linked notes improve retrieval or learning. Search is not on the bench at all, and full-text search would find many of these notes by word. What the bench does show, for certain, is arithmetic about structure: what two steps can and cannot reach, given the links that exist.

Part 3 · covers the anti-lever and the break

A perfect-looking graph, and a silent rename

5 min live

Two levers. The first is a move people make on purpose because the graph looks better afterwards: link every mention of every term. The second is an accident that happens to everyone eventually: notes renamed somewhere that does not update links.

LiveLink every mention: 100 percent recall, 9 percent precision4 min▶

The anti-lever turns every term a note mentions into a link: [[user]], [[interview]], [[client]] and each theme's name. On rung 4 the vault goes from 498 links to 1,488, orphans drop to 0, and the whole vault becomes one connected piece. Recall hits 100 percent and all 12 questions count as answered, because two steps now reach 181.8 notes on average, nearly the entire vault. Precision falls to 9.1 percent, roughly the share of the vault on any one theme: no better than opening notes without looking.

This is the reasoning behind session 1's advice to link the idea, not the word, and it is what session 5 means by asking what the graph view is not telling you. A graph with no orphans and one giant component can be a vault where every question's answer is "everything".

LiveThe rename that no one updated3 min▶

Inside Obsidian a rename is safe: the help says renaming a note updates all the links to it, and with the setting Automatically update internal links turned off, Obsidian prompts you instead. The break models the other case: 20 notes renamed in Finder, in another editor or by a sync script, where nothing can update the links. Every link that pointed at an old name now points at a note that does not exist: 72 of them on rung 4.

The renamed notes keep their own outgoing links, so a backlink still leads to them from the other side, and recall only slips from 84.4 to 80.1 percent. But "interviewer bias" drops from 90.0 to 70.0 percent and stops counting as answered, and "trust and credibility" drops from 26.9 to 3.8. On rung 3, with fewer routes, 51 broken links cut recall from 11.5 to 6.5 percent. Folders and tags do not notice at all. Session 6 is about renaming safely.

Real world

The tidy-up that happened in the file manager. A familiar pattern: someone decides their note titles are inconsistent and fixes them in a batch with a rename tool, outside the note app, on a Sunday. Nothing errors. Weeks later a search for an old project turns up notes whose links lead to empty pages with the old titles. Session 6 shows how to rename from inside Obsidian and how to spot unresolved links afterwards.

Homework

Before session 5

30 min
  1. Write down the ladder from the widget: recall, precision and questions answered for each rung, and one sentence on the rung 3 dip in your own words.
  2. Turn on the anti-lever on every rung and record precision each time. Explain why it ends up almost the same whatever the rung.
  3. In your own vault, pick one theme and ask "find everything about it" from your latest note on it. Count what two clicks reach, then write the map of content that would fix it.
  4. Find three notes in your vault with no links at all. For each, decide: link it up, merge it, or leave it on purpose.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On the bench, rung 3 (idea links only) has a lower mean recall than rung 1 (folders only): 11.5 percent against 19.1. What is the best explanation?

Rung 3's precision is the best of the first three rungs, so the links are good. They are just short corridors. Add maps of content as crossroads and recall jumps to 84.4 percent. Backlinks are counted on every link rung.

2 · After "link every mention of every term", the vault has 0 orphans and one connected component, and recall is 100 percent. Why is that not a better vault?

Orphans and component size are counts of connection, not of meaning. When everything is two steps from everything, the neighbourhood of a note tells you nothing about it. Precision near the share of the vault on one theme is the tell.

3 · Ines renames 20 notes in Finder. What happens to links that pointed at them, and what does the help say happens if she renames inside Obsidian?

A wikilink is a name. Change the name where Obsidian cannot see the change and the name in every link is wrong; on the bench that is 72 broken links on rung 4. The renamed notes keep their own outgoing links, which is why a backlink still reaches them.
