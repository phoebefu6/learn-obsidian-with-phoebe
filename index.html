The six sessions 🧭 · from five linked notes to a vault that lasts

The running case is Ines, a constructed freelance user-research consultant with four active clients (Aldermoor Care, Brightwell Mutual, Dunmore District Council, Corvid Studio) and one part-time contractor, Theo. She keeps seeing the same patterns across clients and cannot find where she wrote them down. Ines, her clients and her vault are invented for teaching, and every page says so. Obsidian is free, and every session uses core features only.

What is honest here 🧾

Linked notes travel with a famous origin story and confident productivity claims. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
What Obsidian does: local Markdown files, wikilinks, backlinks, unlinked mentions, properties, the graph view, core pluginsRead at source in the official Obsidian help, October 2026. Features change: check the current help before relying on a menu name
Luhmann's slip box: about 90,000 cards in two collections, fixed numbers, links, a keyword indexRead at source in Luhmann's own essay (in English translation) and the Bielefeld archive coordinator's 2018 article
"The slip box made Luhmann productive"Contested. One self-reported case, retold by advocates; the archive's own coordinator says the box was not independent of its creator. Session 2 teaches the disagreement
"The graph view is your second brain"Contested. The graph draws notes and links, not relevance; no controlled study was found showing linked notes improve retrieval or learning
Every bench number: recall, precision, notes reached, orphans, components, broken linksComputed by real graph algorithms in your browser on a constructed vault, and reproduced exactly by an independent Python run
